MiRoTV Restore Kit

Android TV / Google TV v0.2.4 Build 16 · Effective / last updated: 13 November 2026

This notice covers MiRoTV Restore Kit, a separate Android TV / Google TV diagnostics, backup-planning and guided recovery companion. It is not a firmware image or part of MiRo TV Edition.

1. Developer and contact

Developer / contact: Mark Eumir Turingan (MiRo Digital Tools). Privacy questions and requests: mygmail@markeumir.xyz. This notice concerns app functionality operated or supplied by MiRo; separately run LAN hosts and third-party platforms may control their own processing.

2. What information the app uses

  • When you initiate a device assessment or recovery workflow, the tool may read installed-package names, versions, settings, compatibility indicators, device model/OS details, diagnostic output, and available backup/snapshot information.
  • Wireless ADB pairing or similar recovery features can involve a local network address, pairing prompts, connection status, and device authorization information. The TV's own permission flow controls access.
  • Device assessments and exported snapshot or recovery-plan files may contain technically sensitive information about installed apps or system configuration.

3. Purpose and lawful basis

Information described above is used to provide the features you choose, remember local preferences, run a requested game session or device workflow, prevent duplicate or inconsistent operations, and respond to support messages. Where EU data-protection law applies to personal information under MiRo's control, the relevant basis may include the legitimate interest in delivering and securing these requested functions (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)) or consent where separately required. This does not authorize unrelated advertising or marketing uses.

4. Network services and sharing

  • The described repair workflow is intended to communicate with the TV or local devices you authorize. Do not upload diagnostic exports, recovery files, or ADB pairing details to public websites.
  • If you voluntarily send logs, screenshots, or reports to the developer for troubleshooting, their contents are used to respond to your support request. Third-party Android, ADB, file-sharing, or device-vendor services have their own policies.

Links to external platforms (such as GitHub Pages, Google Drive, MediaFire, Cloudflare and a device manufacturer's services) are subject to those services' own policies. Web hosting providers may receive routine technical request information, such as IP address and browser details. No advertising data sale is described for these MiRo app functions.

5. Retention

  • Any local reports, exported plans, or system snapshots persist where the user saved them until manually deleted. Pairing/authorization may survive in Android's system settings until revoked.
  • The tool should not be assumed to erase exported files or undo device changes simply because its own app data is cleared.

6. How to delete your data

  • Delete exported assessment or recovery files from the chosen device folder; clear Restore Kit app storage if you want to reset its local settings.
  • In Android/Google TV Developer Options, revoke wireless/USB debugging authorizations when no longer needed. Clear saved pairing entries if offered. Keep recovery backups you still need.
  • If you sent diagnostics in a support request, contact the developer to request deletion of any retained support materials where applicable.

7. Security and permissions

  • Wireless ADB is a powerful device-management capability. Pair only with trusted devices on a controlled network, keep pairing secrets private, and disable debugging when finished.
  • Read-only inspections and proposed recovery actions have different risk levels; verify every requested action and back up before making changes.

Contact mygmail@markeumir.xyz. Some rights have legal limits. If another person operates your LAN host, direct host-side requests to that operator. These apps are not designed to ask for children's real names, addresses, or other sensitive details as a condition of gameplay. Adults supervising family play should choose non-identifying nicknames and avoid sharing screenshots or room links publicly.

This notice reflects the version and features identified above. Any future online, multiplayer, cloud, analytics or permission changes that affect data use will require a policy update. Review the update date at the top for the latest published notice.
