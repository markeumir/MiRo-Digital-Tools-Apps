Millionaires Game — MiRo Edition

In development · Android Build 9 QA / Linux Alpha v0.2.1 · Effective / last updated: 13 November 2026

This notice describes the current experimental MiRo Edition property-trading game and its private LAN hosting prototypes. No public production Android release is currently available.

1. Developer and contact

Developer / contact: Mark Eumir Turingan (MiRo Digital Tools). Privacy questions and requests: mygmail@markeumir.xyz. This notice concerns app functionality operated or supplied by MiRo; separately run LAN hosts and third-party platforms may control their own processing.

2. What information the app uses

  • During LAN games, the chosen host processes player nicknames, room identifiers, connection/reconnect tokens, turn history, dice results, property ownership, virtual cash balances, payments, ledger entries, and saved game progress.
  • Host and clients exchange connection information such as local IP addresses required for LAN play. Saved game and migration backup files may remain on the host.
  • The virtual game currency is not real money. The described game does not require a banking account, card number, or real-money payments.

3. Purpose and lawful basis

Information described above is used to provide the features you choose, remember local preferences, run a requested game session or device workflow, prevent duplicate or inconsistent operations, and respond to support messages. Where EU data-protection law applies to personal information under MiRo's control, the relevant basis may include the legitimate interest in delivering and securing these requested functions (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)) or consent where separately required. This does not authorize unrelated advertising or marketing uses.

4. Network services and sharing

  • Players connected to the same room receive game state and player information needed for multiplayer play. The host operator controls the locally stored game/room records.
  • The current game is designed for local network sessions rather than developer-operated account registration or public cloud matchmaking. A network tunnel, if separately enabled by a host, may involve its provider.

Links to external platforms (such as GitHub Pages, Google Drive, MediaFire, Cloudflare and a device manufacturer's services) are subject to those services' own policies. Web hosting providers may receive routine technical request information, such as IP address and browser details. No advertising data sale is described for these MiRo app functions.

5. Retention

  • Saved rooms, backup copies, and ledger records remain on the selected Linux/Android host until the host operator deletes them or applicable backups expire. Reconnecting may require retained room/player identifiers.
  • Deleting a client's browser cache will not necessarily remove a game saved on the host.

6. How to delete your data

  • Ask the LAN host to delete a saved room and its related backups, subject to host access and backup retention.
  • On a client, clear local game site storage or browser storage to remove locally retained game identifiers. On an Android host, clearing application storage is destructive and may erase all hosted saved games.

7. Security and permissions

  • Use trusted private Wi-Fi/LAN networks. Local browser connections may use HTTP/WebSocket without end-to-end encryption; do not expose experimental rooms publicly without appropriate protection.
  • This product is under QA; behavior and data format may change as the shared engine matures. This notice must be reviewed again before a public release.

8. Your rights and requests

Where applicable under EU/UK privacy law, you may request access, correction, deletion, restriction, or objection concerning personal data for which MiRo is responsible. Contact mygmail@markeumir.xyz. Some rights have legal limits. If another person operates your LAN host, direct host-side requests to that operator. You can also contact your competent data-protection supervisory authority.

9. Children and other users

These apps are not designed to ask for children's real names, addresses, or other sensitive details as a condition of gameplay. Adults supervising family play should choose non-identifying nicknames and avoid sharing screenshots or room links publicly.

10. Changes to this policy

This notice reflects the version and features identified above. Any future online, multiplayer, cloud, analytics or permission changes that affect data use will require a policy update. Review the update date at the top for the latest published notice.
